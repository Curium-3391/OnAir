# Marine VHF (channels 1 to 88, 156 to 162 MHz)

Receive only. Mode list: Maritime > Marine VHF (family 148, engine standard 150). Code: `core/src/marinevhf/`, screens `app/marinevhf/`, channel table `data/bandplans/marine_vhf_channels.csv`.

## What it does

- **Channel picker by number**, with the name and use of every channel ("16 Distress, safety, calling", "13 Bridge to bridge", "22A Coast Guard liaison, MSI"), up / down buttons that skip the data channels (70, 75, 76, 87B, 88B), and a detail panel with the purpose, the ship and coast frequencies, the power limit and the flags (distress, calling, SAR, bridge to bridge, port operations / VTS, intership, public correspondence, safety broadcasts, Coast Guard, low power).
- **Regional plans:** international (ITU Radio Regulations Appendix 18), Europe, Baltic Sea, USA and Canada. The number-to-frequency rule is the ITU one: channels 1 to 28 at 156.050 + 0.05 (n - 1) MHz, channels 60 to 88 at 156.025 + 0.05 (n - 60) MHz; a duplex channel's coast station transmits 4.6 MHz above the ship. Channels 1 to 5, 7, 18 to 28, 60 to 66 and 78 to 86 are duplex; 6, 8 to 17, 67 to 77 are simplex. `A` is the ship frequency used as a simplex channel (USA 22A = 157.100), `B` the coast frequency used as a simplex channel (87B = 161.975 = AIS 1, 88B = 162.025 = AIS 2, Canada 21B and 83B). The USA plan has the lettered channels (1A, 3A, 5A, 7A, 18A ... 83A), duplex 20, 24 to 28 and 84 to 86, and 20A; Europe adds the marina channel M (37, 157.850); the Baltic plan names the Gulf of Finland reporting channels (60 Helsinki, 61 Tallinn, 74 St. Petersburg). Uses are the typical ones; the national rules decide.
- **Narrow FM with squelch:** 16 kHz channel on a 25 kHz raster, +-5 kHz deviation is full scale, 300 to 3000 Hz voice band, a quieting squelch (the discriminator noise above 5.5 kHz against the voice band; it needs no carrier level and opens at the same quality on a weak and a strong signal; the slider is 0 to 100 %), audio delayed by 30 ms so the first syllable is not cut, 150 ms hang. De-emphasis (750 us) or flat audio is a checkbox.
- **Whole-span monitoring:** the carrier finder (`dsp/carrierfind.h`) follows the carriers of the captured span on the channel raster and gives each its own channel receiver (`engine/wide_bank.h`, the same machinery as DMR, P25 and the pagers). An 8 Msps radio (HackRF, Airspy, SDRplay ...) holds the whole band; an RTL-SDR (2.4 Msps) holds about 2 MHz. Up to 10 carriers at once (setCarrierLimit, 1 to 24). The activity list shows every channel of the plan with the level, OPEN / carrier, the number of calls, the total time and when it was last heard; a click listens to that channel.
- **What plays:** the channel you picked; with none picked, the strongest open channel (it stays while it is within 6 dB of the best). With **Watch channel 16** on, an open 16 takes over from whatever plays and the radio goes back when it has been quiet for the hang time.
- **Scan:** choose the channels (all voice, safety, port operations, intership, public correspondence, Coast Guard, or by hand). With a wide radio the scan plays the first of the chosen channels that opens, holds it until it has been quiet for the hang time, and goes on after it; with a narrow radio (some channel outside the span) it steps the radio from channel to channel (dwell per channel, a visit to 16 every 4 s) and stops on activity.
- **Call log per channel:** start time, length, peak and mean level for every transmission, in the Call log tab and as counts in the activity list.
- **Recording:** tick Record and every transmission of every followed channel is saved as `marinevhf_YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS_ch16_156.800MHz.wav` (16-bit mono, 48 kHz; what is heard, with the chosen de-emphasis) in the folder; transmissions under 0.5 s are not kept.
- **Links to DSC and AIS:** channel 70 and the AIS channels are data, not voice: their rows have buttons that open the DSC mode (channel 70, `docs/DSC.md`) and the AIS mode (87B, 88B, 75, 76). In the DSC mode a call that names a VHF working channel ("VHF ch 6") or is a distress alert has a **Listen on VHF channel N** button that opens this mode on that channel (a radio that covers 156 to 162 MHz is needed). Both decoders cannot run at the same time on one radio; the jump restarts the receiver in the new mode.

## Not done

- Duplex channels are followed on both frequencies (ship and coast) as one channel; the call log does not say which side spoke.
- DSC alerts are not shown inside the Marine VHF screen (one receiver mode runs at a time); the jump goes from the DSC screen to this one.
- The channel plan was written from the public regulations (ITU Appendix 18, 47 CFR 80.371 and the like) without being checked against each country's current notices. Corrections to `data/bandplans/marine_vhf_channels.csv` are welcome (the CSV is compiled in; `tests/marinevhf/test_marinevhf_channels.cpp` checks the ITU rule, the pairs and the regional differences).

## Tests

`tests/marinevhf/test_marinevhf_channels.cpp` (every number in every region maps to the right frequencies, duplex pairs, A / B variants, regional plans, lookups, up / down, the data file against the compiled table), `test_marinevhf_scan.cpp` (scan whole-span and single-tuner, dwell, hang, priority; the call log) and `test_marinevhf_rx.cpp` (three NFM channels with noise in one 960 ksps capture: calls, audio tone, recordings, the play choice, scan, priority; one channel at 250 ksps; noise only).
