# Fast scan (Scanner view > Fast scan)

A wideband channel scanner that measures power with an FFT instead of retuning and listening. Code: `core/src/fastscan`, window: `app/scan/fast_scan_ui.cpp`, tests: `tests/fastscan/test_fastscan.cpp`.

**How a pass works.** The band list is cut into hops of the radio's usable bandwidth (RTL-SDR 2.56 Msps, Airspy 10, HackRF 20; the edges the tuner's filter rolls off are dropped). Per hop: retune, discard the settle time, capture one block, average the FFTs of the block (reused window and buffers, no per-hop allocation), calibrate, update the floor, find signals, feed the hit tracker, write the pseudo-spectrum. The next hop is captured while the last is processed. The retune settle is measured at the start ("measure retune"); the profile value is only the first guess.

**Dynamic noise floor.** One floor value per bin, a low-percentile tracker: it falls fast when a sample is below it and rises slowly (default 0.1 dB/s against 0.3 dB/s, so it settles on about the 25th percentile of that bin's noise). A sample more than 6 dB above the floor never raises it, so a carrier that stays does not eat its own threshold; a majority of a hop moving together (a gain change) shifts the whole hop. A running median of every hop's shape removes the hop-edge roll-off and the DC spike. "Keep floor" saves the floor per band and starts from it next time. Thresholds are "N dB above the local floor".

**Hits.** Signals are runs of bins above the floor (a wide signal is one hit with a bandwidth). A hit starts above the threshold, holds above threshold minus 3 dB (hysteresis), needs the minimum duration, and lasts the hang time after the signal is gone. A returning signal is the same row (count +1). Per hit: Tune, Save (memory table, group "Fast scan", mode from a memory row on that frequency or a guess: AM 118-137 MHz, else FM), Lock (ignored from now on), Prio (its hop is visited more often), remove. Memory rows marked locked out or priority count too. Exclusion ranges (FM broadcast, known interferers) skip whole hops and mask bins. Profile presets store bands, step, dwell, threshold, minimum duration, hang, channel raster and recording settings.

**Pseudo-spectrum.** Current, average, max-hold (falls 2 dB/s), the floor and the threshold, all hops stitched; wheel zooms, drag pans, click tunes (snapping to a hit), Ctrl+drag picks a range to scan. A waterfall below uses the app palette.

**Recording hits.** With "record hits" a started hit is recorded from the same capture: a 4x oversampled FFT filterbank (its cost does not grow with the channel count) feeds a small per-channel chain (fine mix, low-pass, FM/AM demodulator) into `scan-recordings/scan_<MHz>MHz_<date>-<time>.wav` (or cf32 + SigMF for IQ). The scan parks on that hop while any recording runs, so the first moments of a hit can be missed when many hops are scanned; a band that fits one hop is watched continuously, up to 100 channels at once. A CPU guard cuts the channel cap when the load passes 70 %; the bar shows "recording N channels". The folder is trimmed to the size budget, oldest first.

**Speed.** With a simulated radio the DSP alone runs at tens of thousands of MHz/s, so it is not the limit. The limit is the retune: usable bandwidth over (settle + capture). Model ceilings at 4 ms dwell: RTL-SDR about 100 MHz/s, Airspy about 670, HackRF about 1070 with libhackrf's retune, about 3400 if the firmware sweep were used. On a HackRF (and Pro) spans over 30 MHz use the firmware sweep (see below). The retune times in the profiles are planning values, not measurements of hardware: the real value is measured when a scan starts and shown in the status line.

## Fast scan v2 (3.2): Whole span and a trustworthy channel list

**Whole span.** One button next to Start sweeps everything the radio can tune (RTL-SDR 25-1700 MHz, Airspy 24-1800, SDRplay 1-2000, HackRF 1-6000, BladeRF/LimeSDR/USRP 70-6000, PlutoSDR 325-3800) with an automatic step, dwell and threshold: spans over 150 MHz get 25-50 kHz bins, a 2 ms dwell and 10-12 dB over the floor; narrow bands keep the values under Advanced ("automatic step and dwell" can be turned off). Model pass times at 2 ms dwell with the planning retune times: RTL-SDR 25-1700 MHz about 15 s (873 hops), Airspy about 2 s, HackRF about 1 s if the firmware sweep were used (about 2.5 s with libhackrf's retune), PlutoSDR about 9 s. The real retune is measured when the scan starts and shown in the status line.

**Confirmed channels.** The list shows only reliable channels by default ("confirmed channels only"). A signal is confirmed when it was seen in at least two separate sweeps (a one-look flash from an overload or a passing burst is not). These are hidden, and shown with a flag when the box is unticked:
- **image**: a signal at the mirrored offset about the hop centre that is at least 18 dB weaker than its twin (the radio's image of a strong station);
- **spur**: a narrow signal present on every look whose level never wobbles (below 0.7 dB over 24+ looks);
- **DC**: a narrow signal on the hop centre (the radio's own DC/IF spike).
Each channel also shows its type (from the bandwidth, the band and whether it is keyed: carrier, AM, NFM, keyed voice, narrow digital, digital, WFM broadcast, wide digital), the number of sweeps it was seen in and how much of the time it was present. A click on a channel tunes and plays it in the mode of its memory row or the guessed one. Code: `core/src/fastscan/qualify.cpp`, tests: `testQualifyPure` and `testQualifyScan` in `tests/fastscan/test_fastscan.cpp` (simulated span with a keyed voice signal and its image, a steady spur, a wide FM carrier and a one-sweep flash).

**HackRF firmware sweep (3.2).** With "HackRF firmware sweep" ticked (Advanced, default) a scan of more than 30 MHz without recording lets the firmware do the hopping: libhackrf's public `hackrf_init_sweep` / `hackrf_start_rx_sweep`, 20 Msps, LINEAR style with a 15 MHz step and a 7.5 MHz offset, so every 16 KiB block (0x7F 0x7F, the tuned frequency as uint64 LE Hz, then int8 IQ) is one 15 MHz hop whose centre is the header frequency plus 7.5 MHz. `FastScanner::runBlocks` feeds each block through the same floor, calibration, hit and qualify code; a pass ends when the hop index wraps. Receive only; no hit recording in this mode (the radio cannot park). If the firmware or library refuses (`init_sweep` error) or the radio is missing, the normal retune loop runs and the status line says why. Code: `core/src/fastscan/fwsweep.cpp`; harness: `dect2fastscan --from 1 --to 6000 --mode fw|retune|both` prints the measured pass time and the confirmed channels.

**Status of the numbers.** The pass times above (and the 3.2 whole-span table) are model figures from the planning retune times. They have NOT been measured on a HackRF: no radio was attached when this was written, so the firmware sweep path is covered by packet-parser, plan and block-loop tests with simulated blocks only. It needs a user test: run `dect2fastscan --from 1 --to 6000 --mode both` with a HackRF or Pro attached and replace the model figures with the printed ones.
